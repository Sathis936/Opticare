10 Simple Ways to Reduce Digital Eye Strain

Table of Contents
  • What Is Digital Eye Strain?
  • Top 10 Tips
  • When to See a Doctor

Most of us now spend eight or more hours a day staring at screens — laptops, phones, tablets and televisions. It is no surprise that "computer vision syndrome", also known as digital eye strain, is one of the most common complaints we hear at OptiCare.

What Is Digital Eye Strain?

Digital eye strain happens when your eyes work harder than normal because of prolonged screen use. Symptoms include sore, tired or dry eyes, blurred vision, headaches and neck or shoulder pain.

The good news? It is almost always preventable with a few simple habits.

"Your eyes were built for the horizon, not for a 14-inch screen. Small breaks make a big difference." — Dr. Meera Nair

Top 10 Tips

1. Follow the 20-20-20 Rule

Every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for at least 20 seconds. It relaxes the focusing muscles of your eyes.

2. Adjust Your Screen Position

Place your monitor about an arm's length away, with the top of the screen at or slightly below eye level.

3. Blink More Often

We blink about a third less than normal when using screens. Consciously blink to keep your eyes lubricated.

4. Use Artificial Tears

Preservative-free lubricating drops can relieve dryness, especially in air-conditioned offices.

5. Optimise Lighting

Reduce glare by positioning screens away from windows and using softer ambient light rather than bright overheads.

6. Increase Text Size

Zoom in until you can read comfortably. Squinting at tiny text forces your eyes to strain.

7. Consider Blue Light Lenses

Blue light filtering lenses can help reduce glare and visual discomfort during long working days.

8. Take Real Breaks

Stand up and walk around every hour. Your eyes and your posture will thank you.

9. Wear Corrective Lenses if Needed

An up-to-date prescription prevents the subtle straining that happens when your eyes try to compensate.

10. Get Regular Eye Exams

A yearly check-up catches small issues before they become big ones.

Taking short breaks keeps your eyes fresh and focused.

When to See a Doctor

If symptoms persist despite these habits, or you notice sudden blurred vision, eye pain or persistent headaches, book an eye test. Persistent strain can be a sign of an underlying prescription change.
